When we first walked the Muddy Fork in the spring of 2022, the bank was failing.
A decade of high water had cut a raw, six-foot scarp into the outside of the
bend, and every storm sent another wheelbarrow's worth of soil into the channel.

## The plan

Restoration here was never going to be one big machine and a single afternoon.
We staked in engineered log structures to slow the water at the toe of the bank,
then planted **1,800 native plugs** — sedges, rushes, and willow stakes — to hold
the soil the logs protected.

> The bank doesn't heal because we planted it. It heals because the plants we
> planted grow roots we will never see.

## What changed

- **Year one:** the willow stakes leafed out; half the sedges took.
- **Year two:** the scarp softened; the first insects came back.
- **Year three:** a volunteer found brook trout holding in the shade line.

None of it was fast. All of it was worth it.
